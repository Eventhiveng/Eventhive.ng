Theme: The Nigeria Opportunity: Capital, Competitiveness and the Future of Growth

8:00am prompt

Nigeria enters 2027 at a defining moment in its economic development. The country is navigating the impacts of major fiscal and monetary reforms while confronting persistent structural constraints, including high operating costs, infrastructure deficits, limited access to affordable capital and pressure on household purchasing power. At the same time, its large domestic market, entrepreneurial base, natural resources and expanding digital economy present significant opportunities for investment and long-term growth.

The central question for 2027 is whether Nigeria can translate economic reforms into greater competitiveness, productive investment, business expansion and improved living standards. Against this backdrop, the Nigeria Economic Breakfast Meeting (NEBM) returns on January 13, 2027, as a high-level economic gathering convening the leaders and institutions shaping Nigeria’s economic future.

Bringing together senior government officials, chief executives, investors, financial institutions, development partners and industry leaders, NEBM 2027 will move beyond conventional outlook presentations towards leadership conversations, cross-sector exchanges and investment-focused dialogue — connecting policy, investment, productivity, competitiveness and inclusive growth.

NEBM 2027 seeks to establish itself as a leading annual convening for Nigeria’s economic decision-makers: a forum for understanding the year ahead, challenging conventional assumptions and translating economic conversations into opportunities for action.

Key Content Tracks

The programme is organised around interconnected economic priorities rather than treating each industry as a separate conversation.

  • Economic Leadership, Reform and Competitiveness

    The next phase of reforms, policy stability, fiscal sustainability and the conditions required to improve productivity and long-term investment.

  • Capital, Investment and the Financing of Growth

    Domestic capital mobilisation, FDI, private equity, infrastructure finance and directing patient capital towards productive sectors.

  • Building a Productive and Competitive Economy

    Manufacturing, agriculture and agro-processing, energy, logistics and value-chain expansion for regional and global supply chains.

  • Infrastructure, Energy and the Cost of Doing Business

    Electricity, transport, digital infrastructure and public-private financing models to close critical gaps and reduce operating costs.

  • Technology, Productivity and the Future of Enterprise

    AI, digital financial services, automation and technology adoption that deliver measurable productivity and new business models.

  • Trade, Regional Integration and Global Positioning

    Export competitiveness, AfCFTA, supply-chain development and expanding non-oil exports and value captured by Nigerian enterprises.

  • Jobs, Skills and Inclusive Prosperity

    Workforce development, youth employment, skills gaps and how private-sector expansion can create meaningful economic opportunity.

Conversation Stream/Keynote

Keynote: Nigeria’s Next Economic Chapter: From Reform to Competitiveness, Investment and Growth

This keynote will examine the strategic choices that will shape Nigeria’s economic trajectory in 2027 and beyond. It will explore what the next phase of reform must deliver, how Nigeria can become a more compelling destination for domestic and international investment, and what is required to unlock productivity across manufacturing, agriculture, energy, finance, technology and trade. Moving beyond headline growth figures, the address will consider the conditions necessary to make growth durable: predictable policy, reliable infrastructure, productive investment, competitive industries and stronger institutional coordination.

Leadership Panel: The Nigeria Growth Compact: What Must Government, Business and Capital Do Differently?

This panel brings together senior policymakers, business leaders and investors to confront the practical choices required to move Nigeria from economic adjustment to sustained growth. The discussion will examine what government must get right to unlock private investment, what businesses must do to raise productivity, and what investors need to commit long-term capital to Nigeria’s productive sectors — with a focus on accountability, execution and measurable outcomes.

  • What must the next phase of economic reform deliver for businesses and investors to feel a tangible difference?
  • Which policy and infrastructure constraints are most urgently holding back productive investment?
  • Where should domestic and international capital be deployed to generate the greatest economic returns?
  • What must Nigerian businesses change to become more productive, export-ready and globally competitive?
  • How can government, business and investors move from shared priorities to concrete commitments and measurable results?
  • What will the political and policy environment mean for Nigeria’s investment outlook in 2027, and how can businesses plan for continuity and growth through a period of political transition?
